# Apple-inspired direction before planning

Read after project inspection and before planning the storyboard. Write `<output-dir>/apple-inspired-direction.md`; this is the shared creative target for the plan, Hyperframes brief, and final-video QA.

## Source and scope

Reference: [Design Apple-style UI](https://aiuxplayground.com/prompts/design-apple-style-ui), AI UX Playground; page updated September 11, 2026, consulted October 2, 2026. This is an independent design prompt, not an Apple specification. The adaptation below is sufficient for routine runs; fetching the page again is optional.

The source emphasizes immediate feedback, direct manipulation, interruptible motion with velocity continuity, restrained spring settling, consistent spatial relationships, purposeful materials, and reduced-motion alternatives. For a recorded demonstration, express these as visible cause and effect, coherent trajectories, and controlled arrivals. Live gesture mechanics require a separate interactive implementation and cannot be verified from a film.

Everything below is a Brag filmmaking adaptation. Do not redesign the source application as part of a video task or invent capabilities. Keep source UI, logo, copy, palette, and font identity recognizable. Prefer quiet presentation and clear product staging by default; explicit user directions override that preference. All seven tone presets, flags, audio options, cue analysis, optional voiceover, share copy, and Hyperframes workflows remain available.

## Establish the direction

Use project inspection evidence to choose the film's focal product moment and its treatment. Record file/component names for product facts; label demonstration data where needed. A landing-page-only project can use its strongest real visual without inventing a user flow.

Specify these decisions concretely:

- **Product and intent:** the audience, outcome to communicate, real UI/flow to show, and why it deserves the centerpiece scene.
- **Visual staging:** exact project colors; heading/body font choices and available fallback; margins and focal scale appropriate to the requested aspect ratio. Preserve product typography; choose compatible presentation typography only where needed.
- **Hierarchy:** the one primary message in each moment, supporting copy, and which source details must stay visible. Use the existing reading-time floors; reserve uncluttered space around the focal UI.
- **Surface treatment:** decide where solid framing, subtle shadow, or translucency helps separate product layers. Record usable contrast and the backdrop. Glass is optional; avoid applying a generic glass skin to the product.
- **Motion arc:** choose the key reveal, a working-app action if available, and the landing/outro. For each, state what moves, its visual origin and destination, whether it overshoots, when it settles, and how long the readable content holds. No selector-level implementation belongs here.
- **Transition logic:** describe what persists across adjacent shots and how the viewer follows it. Use reframing or matched position when useful; allow a deliberate cut when the selected tone needs it.
- **Audio arc:** keep the existing Brag audio selection workflow. Identify visible actions that merit sound and how the bed supports the reveal. Honor disabled audio and deliberate silence.
- **Motion restraint:** record a lower-motion treatment for high-travel or busy moments. This is a fallback design note, not an extra render requirement. If the user requests a reduced-motion film, make it the primary treatment and carry that choice through all artifacts.

Do not equate Apple-inspired with a white palette, Apple logos, San Francisco font downloads, device mockups, or copied launch copy. The product remains the subject.

## Acceptance contract

Before storyboarding, assign stable IDs to a compact set of criteria, with project-specific expected results. The categories below are a starting point, not a scoring shortcut. During planning, map each applicable ID to scenes and planned time windows. Preserve the IDs in the brief and final QA report.

| ID | Category | Make the expected result specific |
|---|---|---|
| D1 | Product truth and identity | Name the exact UI/copy to show and brand details that must remain recognizable. |
| D2 | Message hierarchy and reading | Identify required lines, focal elements, and settled reading holds using Brag's existing floors. |
| D3 | Purposeful movement | Describe the key trajectory and intended landing; list any deliberately energetic exceptions. |
| D4 | Shot continuity | Identify the anchor carried through a transition, or explain the intended cut. |
| D5 | Layer separation | Name the layers and how the focal content stays distinct and legible. |
| D6 | Sound and delivery | Define applicable audible cues, outro hold, chosen format, and requested duration. |

Mark genuinely inapplicable items with a reason, such as no simulated interaction or disabled SFX. User-requested departures are agreed exceptions, not failures. Do not mark a criterion inapplicable merely because it is difficult to achieve.

## Direction document structure

Use these sections in `apple-inspired-direction.md`:

1. **Source and interpretation:** source URL/date, this project's creative premise, and the relevant film adaptation.
2. **Project evidence:** inspected files, product facts, UI or strongest visual, brand colors/fonts, and any assumptions.
3. **Agreed choices:** tone, format, duration, visual staging, hierarchy, materials, motion arc, transition logic, audio posture, and motion-restraint notes.
4. **Acceptance criteria:** ID, expected visible/audible result, applicability, and later scene/time mapping.
5. **Exceptions and unknowns:** explicit user overrides and unresolved facts; omit unsupported claims or choose a source-backed alternative where possible.

**Gate:** The direction exists before the storyboard is finalized. A composer can tell what the film should look and feel like, and a reviewer can identify concrete evidence for the criteria.

## Hyperframes boundary

The direction describes intent and observable results. Hyperframes selects runtime, timing, curves, clip structure, and rendering. Any spring-like filmed movement must be reproducible when seeking directly to a timestamp; it must not depend on real-time input or simulation history. A demonstrated reversal is a scripted video event, not proof of a live interruptible interface.
